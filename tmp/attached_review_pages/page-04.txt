E0 covers only part of the lesion and leaves a large false-negative region, whereas the M4-P prediction aligns more closely with the ground-truth contour and substantially reduces boundary omission in the error overlay. This example is consistent with the aggregate improvement in Table 2: the complete method learns a more comprehensive lesion response and improves coverage while preserving contour continuity. The qualitative case is used only to illustrate model behavior; the overall conclusions remain based on all test samples and paired statistics.The second row of Figure 2 presents two clinically relevant failure modes. Small lesions provide limited foreground signal and may be completely missed after repeated downsampling. Conversely, a localized abnormal response on a truly empty slice can produce a large false positive and increase the burden of manual review. These opposing errors correspond to insufficient Recall and degraded Precision or empty-slice false-positive rate, respectively, demonstrating that Dice or overall pixel accuracy alone can obscure practical risk. Future work could jointly investigate small-lesion resampling, scale- or boundary-sensitive losses, hard-example mining, and threshold calibration, while carefully assessing whether connected-component postprocessing removes genuine small lesions.Comparison of E0 and M4-P and representative failure casesTraining randomness and patient variability reflect optimization uncertainty and case-level heterogeneity, respectively, and both the quantitative and qualitative results show some patient dependence. Multi-seed and patient-level paired summaries indicate that improvements from the complete method and ResNet34 are the most consistent. Benefits from the other components may depend on lesion size, image quality, and channel characteristics. Future studies should expand the patient cohort and conduct prespecified subgroup analyses.V. Conclusion and Future WorkUnder a fixed patient-disjoint split and matched three-seed experiments, M4-P achieved the highest P-MIoU (0.7664) and P-Dice (0.8425). The ResNet34 encoder produced the largest and most stable gain, whereas three-channel input, light augmentation, and ImageNet pretraining provided smaller positive effects. This study is limited to one relatively small two-dimensional dataset and lacks external and expert validation. Future work will evaluate multicenter data, three-dimensional context, missing-modality modeling, and calibration of severe false positives and false negatives.ReferencesGHADIMI D J, VAHDANI A M, KARIMI H, et al.
