Brain Tumor Slice Segmentation Using Multi-Channel MRI and ResNet34-U-NetDewen Zhang*School of Computer SoftwareTianjin UniversityTianjin China2006devinchang@gmail.comAbstract—Accurate automatic segmentation of brain tumors in magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is clinically important for quantitative image analysis. However, multichannel input, data augmentation, network architecture, and pretraining are often introduced simultaneously in existing deep learning systems, leaving the independent contribution of each component insufficiently validated. To address this issue, we conducted controlled experiments on the kaggle_3m dataset using a unified patient-level split that strictly prevents data leakage. Starting from a standard U-Net baseline, we performed paired ablation experiments across multiple random seeds to independently evaluate three-channel input, light augmentation, a ResNet34 encoder, and ImageNet pretraining. The complete M4-P configuration achieved the best overall test performance, with a Positive Macro IoU of 0.7664 and a Positive Dice score of 0.8425. The ResNet34 encoder provided the most stable improvement, while the remaining components showed auxiliary positive trends. Together with qualitative error analysis, these results provide reliable evidence for component selection and optimization in brain tumor segmentation models.Keywords-Brain tumor segmentation; Magnetic resonance imaging; Binary semantic segmentation; U-Net; ResNet34; Ablation study IntroductionAccurate segmentation of brain tumors in multichannel MRI slices has substantial clinical value, yet manual delineation is time-consuming and sensitive to image quality. Blurred lesion boundaries, scale variation, and inter-observer variability further complicate contour definition. Automatic segmentation can provide reproducible initial contours for lesion localization, quantitative analysis, and expert review [1][2].Although deep learning is now widely used, the complementary value of multichannel information is not fully exploited under all data conditions. Moreover, input design, augmentation, encoder architecture, and pretraining are often introduced as a combined package, making their individual effects difficult to isolate. Inadequate data organization and validation can further undermine the reliability of comparisons. We therefore construct paired component-level controls under a unified experimental protocol to assess the overall benefit of the complete method, the contribution of each component, and the consistency of these effects across training states and patients.We perform binary lesion-versus-background segmentation on two-dimensional MRI slices from the kaggle_3m dataset using a unified patient-disjoint split. Although the dataset labels are designated as low-grade glioma (LGG), this study does not address multiclass LGG classification or tumor subregion segmentation.The main contributions of this study are threefold. First, we establish a fixed cleaning checklist and patient-isolated splits to unify all samples used for training, validation, and testing. Second, we conduct paired ablation studies across multiple random seeds to evaluate the individual effects of three-channel inputs, lightweight data augmentation, a ResNet34 encoder, and ImageNet pre-training. Third, we demonstrate that the integrated scheme achieves overall performance gains, with the ResNet34 encoder delivering the most consistent positive impact and the remaining components showing supplementary positive signals.Related WorkU-Net remains a standard architecture for brain tumor segmentation [4][9]. Subsequent studies have investigated residual encoders and attention mechanisms [5][15], transfer learning [6], and multimodal MRI input [3]. However, these components are often evaluated jointly, obscuring their individual contributions. Moreover, correlated slices from the same patient can cause data leakage when partitions are not patient-disjoint [8]. We therefore evaluate three-channel input, light augmentation, a ResNet34 encoder, and ImageNet pretraining under a fixed patient-level split and matched multi-seed protocol, using overlap, false-positive, and patient-level analyses.MethodologyDataset and Curation ChecklistWe use the public LGG Segmentation Dataset from Kaggle Brain MRI Segmentation [16]. The data originate from the TCIA TCGA low-grade glioma collection and include MRI slices with manually annotated FLAIR abnormality masks under a CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license. The fixed curated manifest contains 3,629 slices from 104 LGG patients. The patient-disjoint training, validation, and test partitions contain 2,619, 485, and 525 slices from 85, 9, and 10 patients, respectively; the corresponding positive-mask counts are 935, 167, and 173, and the empty-mask counts are 1,684, 318, and 352.
