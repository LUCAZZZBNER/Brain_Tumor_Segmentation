Its stronger hierarchical representation and multiscale encoding preserve both lesion semantics and boundary information, making it the core architectural improvement in the complete method. Because this comparison changes the encoder configuration as a whole, the conclusion concerns the combined effect of the ResNet34 design rather than any individual internal block.The mean effects of three-channel input, light augmentation, and ImageNet pretraining are all positive, indicating auxiliary beneficial signals. The three-channel input provides additional image information for some seeds and patients, although its average gain is modest. Light geometric perturbations offer regularization, and further optimization of transformation type and strength may improve stability. Pretraining is beneficial in most paired comparisons, suggesting that a stronger initialization can aid optimization; its effect nevertheless depends on the domain gap between natural images and MRI, channel mapping, and the fine-tuning strategy.Component contributions are not simply additive. Input design, augmentation, the encoder, and initialization may be complementary, redundant, or conditionally dependent, and paired effects can vary with the training state and patient. Current evidence identifies ResNet34 as the stable core, while the other three components show positive tendencies consistent with the complete method and warrant further development. Complete multimodal data, augmentation search, medical-image pretraining, and staged fine-tuning could be used to validate and amplify these signals.
